# Data request: midwater trawl biological samples, Pacific Spiny Dogfish

**Requester:** S.D.N. Johnson, Landmark Fisheries Research
**Date:** 25 August 2026
**Purpose:** Independent review of the Outside Pacific Spiny Dogfish
assessment (Anderson et al. 2026, CSAS Res. Doc. 2025/055) in support of
rebuilding planning
**Suggested route:** Groundfish Data Unit, Pacific Biological Station
(GFBio commercial biological samples)

---

## Background

An independent review of the above assessment flagged an anomalous
sex-length pattern in the fitted length compositions, in which
observations coded female are concentrated at smaller lengths than those
coded male. We have verified the numeric sex-code dictionary against the
DFO groundfish data dictionary published on open.canada.ca (1 = male,
2 = female) and confirmed that the survey extraction reproduces the
published open-data records. The survey component of the question is
therefore closed.

The pattern persists, materially, in one fleet only: midwater trawl, where
mean length of female-coded specimens is 7.4 cm below that of male-coded
specimens. Midwater trawl samples are commercial rather than survey data
and are not available through the open data portal. We are seeking these
records to complete the audit and to characterise the size-selective
availability that we believe explains the pattern.

## Records requested

Commercial biological samples for North Pacific Spiny Dogfish
(*Squalus suckleyi*), gear type MIDWATER TRAWL, outside waters
(excluding Major Statistical Area 4B):

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Species | North Pacific Spiny Dogfish (*Squalus suckleyi*) |
| Gear | Midwater trawl |
| Area | Outside stock; excluding 4B |
| Years | 1979 to present (2019 is the latest in our current copy) |
| Sampling description | UNSORTED, plus KEEPERS and DISCARDS if held |

Fields required, per specimen:

- Sample and specimen identifiers
- Year, month, and Major Statistical Area
- Length, with the length type recorded (total, fork, or second dorsal)
  and the measurement protocol
- Sex code, together with the sex-code dictionary applicable to
  commercial samples
- Maturity code and maturity convention, where recorded
- Usability code
- Sampling description (unsorted, keepers, discards)

## Preferred form, and a confidentiality-safe alternative

Specimen-level records are preferred. We recognise that commercial
fishery data may be subject to vessel-confidentiality screening. If
specimen-level release is constrained, the following aggregation would
meet our need without identifying vessels or trips:

> Counts of specimens by year, sex, and 5 cm length bin, pooled across
> all vessels and trips within each year, for the gear and area above.

This is the form in which the data enter the assessment, so aggregation to
this level would not reduce its usefulness for the audit.

## Specific questions the data would resolve

1. Is the commercial sex-code dictionary identical to the survey
   dictionary (1 = male, 2 = female)? Our evidence indicates it is, but it
   has not been confirmed from a documented source for commercial samples
   specifically, as it now has for surveys.
2. Does the female-smaller pattern in midwater trawl hold across years, or
   is it driven by a small number of large samples? Our copy holds 27,933
   unsorted specimens across only 257 distinct samples, 18,741 of them in
   the 1990s, so a few sampling events may dominate.
3. Are the midwater trawl samples drawn disproportionately from juvenile
   aggregations, which would explain the pattern as availability rather
   than as a data error?

## Context on why this matters

The assessment attributes essentially all landed catch to females through
strongly sex-specific selectivity. Whether that structure is necessary, or
is absorbing availability and sampling effects, bears directly on
estimated female fishing mortality and therefore on rebuilding advice. The
midwater trawl compositions are the most informative data for
distinguishing those explanations.
